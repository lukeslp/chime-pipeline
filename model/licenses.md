# Corpus sources and licenses

Checked on 2026-09-25 and 2026-10-03 against the pages named under each entry (the date is on each one), before anything was downloaded. Every source below is under a license that allows redistribution and derivative works with attribution (Apache-2.0, CC BY 4.0). Anything with a NonCommercial clause, an unclear license or no license was refused, and so was anything written by a model and offered as human text.

Four of the five sources are human-written (NUS SMS, Taskmaster-1, OASST2 and Taskmaster-3); SODA is synthetic dialogue generated by a language model. The human-written share of the assembled corpus is stated in `stats.json` under `human_written`.

Two builds use these sources. `corpus/` (v2, built 2026-09-25) is NUS SMS, Taskmaster-1 and SODA, and its own `LICENSES.md` lists only those three. `corpus-v3/` (built 2026-10-03, `corpus.py --preset v3`) adds OASST2 and Taskmaster-3. This file is the v3 `LICENSES.md`, which `corpus.py` writes from the same records, so it covers all five.

## SODA (allenai/soda)

- License: CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
- Where: https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/soda
- Version: Hugging Face dataset revision fdc848ab0183208ea7808206c91c724414d0a071 (2023-01-04)
- Taken: every turn of the `dialogue` list in train, valid and test parquet files; both speakers are story characters, so there is no assistant side to drop
- Origin: synthetic: dialogues generated by GPT-3.5 (InstructGPT) from commonsense triples, per the SODA paper; nothing in it was typed by a person
- License read at: dataset card front matter `license: cc-by-4.0` and the hub tag `license:cc-by-4.0` on the record above
- Verified on: 2026-09-25
- Terms beyond the license: checked on 2026-10-03. The card at the pinned revision states `license: cc-by-4.0` and `annotation_creators: machine-generated` and adds no other terms. OpenAI's terms are a contract between OpenAI and its API customer, here the SODA authors: the API Terms of Use in force when SODA was published (updated November 3, 2022; read from the Wayback Machine capture of https://openai.com/api/policies/terms/ of 2022-12-08) assign the Output to the customer, forbid the customer to "use the Services to develop foundation models or other large scale models that compete with OpenAI" and to "represent that output from the Services was human-generated when it is not"; the current Terms of Use (effective January 1, 2026; Wayback capture of https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/ of 2026-10-03, the live page refuses scripted reads) say "Use Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI" and keep the human-generated rule. LocalType never used OpenAI's services, so those terms do not bind it; it takes SODA under CC BY 4.0 from its publisher. Read as broadly as possible, a 2M-parameter on-device next-word model is neither a foundation model nor a large-scale model and does not compete with OpenAI. The one term worth honouring anyway is the labelling one: SODA is marked model-generated everywhere here and never counted as human-written. This is my reading, not legal advice.

## Refused

- HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk, everyday-conversations subset: used in an earlier build and dropped: the subset is HuggingFaceTB/everyday-conversations-llama3.1-2k, generated with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, so its `user` turns are model text, not human-written as first labelled, and the Llama 3.1 license attaches a naming condition to models trained on its outputs
- HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk subsets other than everyday-conversations: synthetic as well, and instruction, rewriting, summarization, constraint, code and long-document prompts, the wrong register for a keyboard
- Taskmaster-1 woz-dialogs.json: CC BY 4.0 but spoken transcripts, not written text
- NUS SMS Corpus Chinese portion: not English
- PersonaChat (ParlAI personachat.tgz, sha256 507cf8641d333240654798870ea584d854ab5261071c5e3521c20d8fa41d5622): license unclear (checked 2026-10-03): human-typed by paired Mechanical Turk workers, but its publisher states no license for the data. https://parl.ai/docs/tasks.html and https://parl.ai/projects/personachat/ name none; the archive holds only data files; the paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07243) says the data is "available open source in ParlAI" without naming a license; ParlAI's MIT license (https://github.com/facebookresearch/ParlAI/blob/main/LICENSE) covers "this source code" in the repository, while parlai/tasks/personachat/build.py downloads the data from http://parl.ai/downloads/personachat/personachat.tgz; https://huggingface.co/datasets/convai-challenge/conv_ai_2 says "unknown". A written license statement from the publisher would admit it
- Switchboard and the Charlotte narratives from the Open ANC (OANC-1.0.1-UTF8.zip, sha256 1a5c37b0227c176d2c5a00f23721e70e7489782e31ba14e58e47f56eff04d24f): license unclear and spoken (checked 2026-10-03): https://anc.org/data/oanc/ says the OANC is "unrestricted in terms of usage and redistribution", but OANC/license.txt inside the 1.0.1 archive is a non-exclusive, non-transferable end-user license (Switchboard data copyright the Trustees of the University of Pennsylvania) that forbids distributing substantively modified versions without written permission and asks for deletion when a project goes inactive; the two statements conflict. Both are also transcribed speech (lowercase, unpunctuated, with fillers and repetitions), refused for the same reason as the Taskmaster-1 spoken dialogs
- Taskmaster-2 (TM-2-2020): CC BY 4.0 but spoken (checked 2026-10-03): the user turns are transcribed from recordings; https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Taskmaster/blob/master/TM-2-2020/README.md says "Taskmaster-2 consists entirely of spoken two-person dialogs"
- QuAC (allenai/quac): license conflict (checked 2026-10-03): https://quac.ai/ distributes it under CC BY-SA 4.0 while the Hugging Face mirror says MIT; the primary page wins, and share-alike would bind the assembled corpus, which needs a decision of its own. The register (questions about a hidden Wikipedia section) adds little for a keyboard
- OASST2 assistant turns, and prompter turns flagged synthetic, deleted or failed review: assistant turns are written in an assistant's register, not what people type on a phone; flagged turns are model-written or were rejected by the dataset's own reviewers
- Taskmaster-3 agent turns: the ticketing agent's side, an assistant's register; only the customer side is taken, as with Taskmaster-1
